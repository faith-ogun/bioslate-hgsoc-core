## Report on CDK5 – PI4KB Gene Pair

### 1. Background on Genes

**CDK5**: CDK5 (cyclin-dependent kinase 5) is predominantly known for its role in the central nervous system, particularly in neuronal cell signaling rather than cell cycle regulation, distinguishing it from other cyclin-dependent kinases. Its dysregulation has been implicated in neurodegenerative disorders like Alzheimer's disease but also in various cancers, such as ovarian cancer, where it has been observed to affect processes like mitochondrial fission and cell proliferation ([PubMed ID: 40384022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40384022)).

**PI4KB**: PI4KB (phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase beta) is involved in intracellular signaling pathways linked to membrane trafficking and lipid metabolism. Emerging evidence suggests its role in cancer, particularly RAS-driven cancers where PI4KB participates in a distinct form of autophagy critical for cancer cell survival ([PubMed ID: 39988733](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39988733)). Despite its implications in cancer, detailed studies on its cancer biomarker or direct involvement have been limited.

### 5. Translational Potential (across cancers)

**CDK5** has shown potential in ovarian cancer treatment strategies, where its inhibition through compounds like olaparib modulates mitochondrial dynamics and cancer cell survival pathways ([PubMed ID: 40384022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40384022)).

**PI4KB** involvement in the P38-ULK1-PI4KB signaling axis suggests its relevance in targeting RAS-driven malignancies, especially given its distinct role in non-canonical autophagy, a process vital for the survival of RAS-mutated cancers ([PubMed ID: 39988733](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39988733)).

### 6. Conclusion

The genetic pair of CDK5 and PI4KB presents an intriguing, albeit under-explored, potential in cancer biology and treatment. While CDK5 is established in the neuronal context, its emerging roles in cancer pathophysiology warrant further investigation, particularly in conjunction with compounds like olaparib in breast and ovarian cancers. PI4KB's involvement in non-canonical autophagy indirectly links it to cancer pathways and establishes a basis for future research focusing on RAS-dependent cancers. Drug targeting progress remains limited, emphasizing a need for continued research on both genes to harness their full therapeutic potential.
