# SCARA3 – SPAG5 Gene Pair: A Structured Report

## Background on Genes

### SCARA3
SCARA3 is a member of the scavenger receptor family, playing roles in cancer by influencing cellular processes such as oxidative stress response and immune regulation. SCARA3 has been linked to cervical cancer, glioblastoma, and colorectal cancer as a prognostic biomarker. Specifically, SCARA3 mRNA is significantly overexpressed in ovarian carcinoma compared with breast carcinoma effusions, indicating its potential role in the distinction between these cancer types ([PMID: 21855113](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21855113)).

### SPAG5
SPAG5 is involved in spindle formation during cell division and has been regarded as an oncogene in several cancers. It is significantly overexpressed in ovarian cancer and breast cancer, being associated with poor prognosis and advanced tumor stages ([PMID: 31985007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31985007)).

## Translational Potential (across cancers)

- **SCARA3** has been implicated in several cancer types beyond ovarian cancer, including cervical and colorectal cancers. However, its role appears predominantly as a prognostic marker rather than a therapeutic target.
- **SPAG5** demonstrates potential as a therapeutic target due to its involvement in breast cancer, ovarian cancer, and possibly AML, suggesting SPAG5's importance across a spectrum of malignancies. 

## Conclusion

Despite the absence of direct synthetic lethality interactions between SCARA3 and SPAG5, both genes hold significant potential as prognostic biomarkers across various cancers, with SCARA3 prominently expressed in ovarian carcinoma and SPAG5 associated with poorer outcomes in ovarian and breast cancers. Further research is needed to explore potential therapeutic targeting of SPAG5, especially given its oncogenic role and expression patterns in several cancer types.
