# ASAH1 – TAFAZZIN Report

## 1. Background on Genes

### ASAH1
The ASAH1 gene encodes the enzyme acid ceramidase, which is involved in sphingolipid metabolism. Acid ceramidase has been implicated in cancer due to its role in cell proliferation and apoptosis regulation. It has been identified as a prognostic marker in various cancers, including colorectal and ovarian cancer. ASAH1 expression is associated with better prognosis in specific histotypes of ovarian cancer such as endometrioid and clear cell types (PMID: 31897818).

### TAFAZZIN
The TAFAZZIN gene encodes a phospholipid-lysophospholipid transacylase that is crucial for mitochondrial function. It is notably associated with Barth syndrome, which includes cardiomyopathy and neutropenia, conditions that can influence cancer therapy outcomes. While its direct role in cancer is less clearly defined, mitochondrial dysfunction associated with TAFAZZIN mutations can contribute to metabolic disturbances relevant in cancer contexts (PMID: 40603434).

## 5. Translational Potential (across cancers)

There is currently insufficient data regarding the translational potential of ASAH1 and TAFAZZIN in non-ovarian/breast cancers, given the lack of specific SL evidence or targeted therapies.

## 6. Conclusion

Although ASAH1 and TAFAZZIN play roles in significant biological pathways relevant to cancer, the specific interaction between these genes and their potential as a target for synthetic lethality have yet to be explicitly established in the literature. Clinical trials related to these genes focus on conditions like Farber and Barth syndromes, indicating broader implications for metabolic and mitochondrial diseases rather than direct cancer targeting at this time.
