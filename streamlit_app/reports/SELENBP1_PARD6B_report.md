# Report on the Gene Pair: SELENBP1 – PARD6B

## Background on Genes

### SELENBP1
Selenium-binding protein 1 (SELENBP1) is implicated in various cancers. SELENBP1 is suggested to suppress malignant progression, such as in nasopharyngeal carcinoma, via the KEAP1-NRF2 signaling pathway (PMID: [40632190](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40632190)). It is also involved in the immune regulation processes seen in diseases like sepsis, potentially influencing cancer growth through immunological dysregulation (PMID: [40298842](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40298842)). Furthermore, its expression correlates with reduced tumor growth and progression in multiple cancers (PMID: [23500078](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23500078)).

### PARD6B
Partitioning Defective 6B (PARD6B) acts as a scaffold in epithelial cell polarity and highlights a role in tumor growth, particularly in colorectal cancer (CRC). Its high expression is associated with increased MYC expression, contributing to tumor progression, with increased prevalence in gastrointestinal cancers and a connection to poor prognosis (PMID: [40533910](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40533910)). PARD6B also contributes to treatment resistance, influencing outcomes in chemoresistance for cancers such as ovarian cancer (PMID: [39262489](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39262489)).

## Translational Potential (across cancers)

Currently, no SL evidence or drugs have been directly identified for SELENBP1 and PARD6B beyond ovarian and breast cancers. Both genes show implications in various cancers, including colorectal carcinoma for PARD6B and multiple malignancies for SELENBP1, primarily through indirect mechanisms such as affecting pathways known in radio-resistant breast cancer (PMID: [38823535](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38823535)).

## Conclusion

The exploration of SELENBP1 and PARD6B indicates important but separate roles in cancer biology, with no direct evidence of synthetic lethality. SELENBP1 is largely involved in tumor suppression and immune regulation across various cancers. In contrast, PARD6B contributes to cellular polarity and tumor progression, particularly in colorectal cancer. The absence of targeted drugs and clinical trials for both genes underscores the need for further research into their potential interactions and therapeutic targeting.
