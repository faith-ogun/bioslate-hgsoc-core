# PRKAG2 – PI4KB Report

## 1. Background on Genes

### PRKAG2
PRKAG2 encodes the gamma-2 non-catalytic subunit of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), a key regulator of energy balance at both the cellular and whole-body levels. AMPK activation plays an important role in metabolic processes and has been implicated in cancer, cardiovascular diseases, and other conditions. Notably, aberrations in PRKAG2 have been associated with the risk of cardiovascular toxicity in patients undergoing chemotherapy, which highlights the gene's potential relevance in the context of cancer treatment (PMID: [40152040](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40152040)).

### PI4KB
PI4KB encodes phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase beta, an enzyme involved in the synthesis of phosphatidylinositides, which play a vital role in membrane trafficking and signaling. It has demonstrated a role in the growth and metastasis of lung cancer, by influencing the localization and function of oncogenic and tumor suppressor pathways (PMID: [40189704](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40189704)). Furthermore, PI4KB activity was noted in non-canonical autophagy forms, which are critical for the survival of RAS-mutated cancer cells, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target (PMID: [40055523](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055523)).

## 5. Translational Potential (across cancers)

The modulation of PI4KB activity in non-canonical autophagy processes provides a promising avenue for targeting RAS-mutated cancers (PMID: [39988733](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39988733)). This highlights PI4KB's impact beyond any single cancer type, offering potential applications across various malignancies with similar oncogenic profiles.

## 6. Conclusion

While no direct synthetic lethality evidence was found for PRKAG2 and PI4KB, both genes are implicated in cancer biology through distinct pathways. PRKAG2 is mainly associated with metabolic regulation and has potential implications in chemotherapy-induced toxicity, whereas PI4KB plays a more direct role in oncogenic pathways and cancer survival mechanisms. Exploring targeted therapies for these genes could enhance cancer treatment strategies, particularly in metabolic and RAS-mutated contexts.
