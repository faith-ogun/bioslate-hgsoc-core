# CD47 – TTF2 Gene Pair Detailed Report

## 1. Background on Genes

**CD47:**
CD47 is a protein that is ubiquitously expressed on the surface of cells and is involved in cellular self-recognition, preventing phagocytosis by binding to signal regulatory protein alpha (SIRPα) on macrophages. It is identified as a critical target in the treatment of various cancers due to its role in the "don't eat me" signal that allows cancer cells to avoid immune destruction. Overexpression of CD47 is noted in various types of cancer, including ovarian cancer, and it is being explored as a biomarker and therapeutic target ([PMID: 40384520](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40384520)).

**TTF2:**
TTF2 (Transcription Termination Factor 2) is involved in terminating transcription during the process of gene expression and plays a role in cell cycle regulation. It has been detected to be overexpressed in several solid tumors and is associated with poor survival outcomes. TTF2 is involved in cell cycle arrest during mitosis ([PMID: 40410652](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40410652)).
