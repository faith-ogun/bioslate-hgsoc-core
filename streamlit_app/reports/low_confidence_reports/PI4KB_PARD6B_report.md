# PI4KB – PARD6B Gene Pair: A Clinical and Research Report

## Background on Genes

### PI4KB
Phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase beta (PI4KB) plays a crucial role in the synthesis of phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate (PI4P) involved in cell signaling and membrane trafficking. In the context of cancer, PI4KB has been implicated in oncogenesis, particularly through its involvement in autophagy and survival of RAS-mutant tumor cells. Studies have shown that PI4KB phosphorylation is crucial in the regulation of non-canonical autophagy pathways, which are essential for the survival of RAS-mutated cancers such as colorectal and pancreatic cancers (PMID: 40055523, PMID: 39988733).

### PARD6B
Partitioning defective 6 homolog beta (PARD6B) is a member of the Par protein complex involved in cell polarity and intracellular signaling. In cancer, PARD6B is associated with tumor growth and progression; recent studies indicate its role in colorectal cancer, where it helps regulate MYC expression and is considered a poor prognostic marker (PMID: 40533910). Evidence also suggests an association with chemoresistance in ovarian cancer (PMID: 39262489).

## Translational Potential (across cancers)

While PI4KB's role in RAS-mutated cancers, particularly colorectal and pancreatic cancers, establishes its involvement in cancer progression through unique autophagy mechanisms (PMID: 40055523, PMID: 39988733), its potential as an isolated therapeutic target remains unexplored in other cancer types. PARD6B is noted for its role in colorectal and ovarian cancers but lacks experimental or clinical investigation as a synthetic lethal partner with PI4KB across different malignancies.

## Conclusion

While independent roles of PI4KB and PARD6B are significant in cancer biology, establishing a synthetic lethality relationship remains elusive with current data. PI4KB's regulatory role in RAS-driven autophagy presents a potential target for cancers harboring RAS mutations. PARD6B's function as a prognostic marker in colorectal and ovarian cancers underpins its importance. However, a concerted examination targeting a possible synthetic lethal interplay in diverse cancer types could usher new avenues for therapeutic interventions.
