# RBM33 – PI4KB Gene Pair Report

## 1. Background on Genes

### RBM33
RBM33 is identified as a novel RNA-binding protein involved in the regulation of N-6-methyladenosine (m<sup>6</sup>A) RNA methylation, which plays a crucial role in gene expression regulation. This protein forms a complex with ALKBH5 and contributes to the demethylation of specific mRNA transcripts, affecting tumorigenesis. Specifically, RBM33 has been implicated in the oncogenesis of head-neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) by promoting autophagy and stabilizing DDIT4 mRNA, highlighting its significance in cancer biology (PMID: [37257451](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37257451), [37327770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37327770)).

### PI4KB
PI4KB is a phosphatidylinositol kinase involved in non-canonical autophagy, specifically linked to the proliferation and survival of RAS-mutated cancer cells via the P38-ULK1-PI4KB axis. Its phosphorylation is a targetable feature in RAS-driven cancers, providing a potential therapeutic strategy (PMID: [40189704](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40189704), [40055523](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40055523)).

The evidence of PI4KB involvement in non-canonical autophagy provides significant translational potential across various cancer types, particularly those driven by RAS mutations, suggesting the therapeutic targeting of the P38-ULK1-PI4KB axis.

## 6. Conclusion
RBM33 and PI4KB play roles in cancer pathophysiology through RNA methylation and non-canonical autophagy, respectively.
