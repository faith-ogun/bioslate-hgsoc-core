# Report on Gene Pair: TPMT – EXOC5

## 1. Background on Genes

**TPMT (Thiopurine S-methyltransferase):**

TPMT is an enzyme that plays a critical role in the metabolism of thiopurine drugs, which are used in the treatment of various cancers and autoimmune diseases. It has been linked to drug-induced toxicities, particularly with thiopurine drugs like azathioprine. TPMT genetic polymorphisms are significant in pharmacogenomics as they affect enzyme activity, impact drug efficacy, and the risk of adverse drug reactions (PMID: 40634300, PMID: 40149251).

Relevance to Cancer: TPMT's activity influences the effectiveness and toxicity of chemotherapeutic agents, and understanding its genetic variations aids in optimizing cancer treatment, particularly in pediatric oncology (PMID: 40222694). It is also implicated in ovarian cancer prognosis through its expression levels (PMID: 34322485).

**EXOC5 (Exocyst Complex Component 5):**

EXOC5 is part of the exocyst complex involved in various cellular processes including vesicular trafficking, protein translocation, and ciliogenesis. Altered expression and function of EXOC5 have been associated with tumorigenesis and metastasis. Notably, it is involved in the process of renal ciliogenesis and has been shown to affect breast cancer cell dynamics through the modulation of E-cadherin, impacting cancer progression and metastasis (PMID: 30824539, PMID: 22638108, PMID: 21036701).

Relevance to Cancer: EXOC5 is implicated in the mechanics of inflammatory breast cancer, affecting cell adhesion properties and potentially influencing metastatic capabilities.

## 5. Translational Potential (across cancers)

Current insights suggest TPMT's pharmacogenetics plays a crucial role in treating various cancers by optimizing chemotherapy protocols, minimizing toxicity, and improving patient outcomes. Its relevance across different cancer types, particularly in pediatrics, makes it a significant gene in cancer pharmacogenomics (PMID: 40149251).

EXOC5's involvement in cell adhesion and metastasis implies potential translational applications in understanding cancer cell behavior and developing therapeutic interventions that target these pathways, particularly in treatment-resistant forms such as inflammatory breast cancer (PMID: 21036701).
