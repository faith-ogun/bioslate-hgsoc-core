# Report on CD47 – ACLY Gene Pair

## 1. Background on Genes

### CD47
CD47 is a transmembrane protein often referred to as a "don't eat me" signal, as it interacts with signal regulatory protein alpha (SIRPα) to inhibit phagocytosis by macrophages. Overexpression of CD47 is noted in various cancers, making it a significant target for cancer immunotherapy. It is involved in several recent studies, emphasizing its role in immuno-oncology. For instance, CD47 is recognized both as a biomarker and a therapeutic target in ovarian cancer, highlighting its potential role in tumor biology (PMID: 40384520). Additionally, it has implications in metabolic regulation and cancer therapy landscapes (PMID: 40630093).

### ACLY
ATP citrate lyase (ACLY) is a key enzyme in the conversion of citrate to acetyl-CoA, pivotal in fatty acid biosynthesis. ACLY overexpression is linked to cancer progression and poor prognosis, particularly due to its role in metabolic reprogramming in cancer cells. Its inhibition has been shown to reduce tumor growth and overcome drug resistance, such as cisplatin in ovarian cancer (PMID: 33816292).

## 5. Translational Potential (across cancers)

While there is significant potential for CD47 as a therapeutic target across a variety of cancers including triple-negative breast cancer and ovarian cancer (PMID: 40384520, PMID: 33816292), the lack of targeted ACLY inhibitors in active oncology trials limits its current translational potential beyond preclinical findings.

## 6. Conclusion

CD47 emerges as a viable immunotherapeutic target in several cancers, with a growing number of monoclonal antibodies entering clinical evaluation. ACLY, while crucial in cancer metabolism, lacks active clinical investigations specifically in cancer, though it remains an area of metabolic intervention interest. The promising preclinical results for CD47 blockade call for further focused clinical assessments, possibly leveraging ACLY in combination strategies.
