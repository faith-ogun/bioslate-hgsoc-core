## 1. Background on Genes

### RFX5
RFX5 (Regulatory Factor X5) is a transcription factor known to play a critical role in immune function and has been implicated in the progression of various cancer types, including breast cancer. It was identified as a part of an immune-related signature predictive of lymph node metastasis in breast cancer, and its expression correlates with poor prognosis in ACT-BC patients, suggesting its contribution to cancer progression (PMID: 40520263, PMID: 40356407, PMID: 40220043).

### PARD6B
PARD6B (Partitioning Defective 6B) is a polarity gene involved in establishing epithelial cell polarity and is linked to cancer cell proliferation. In colorectal cancer, PARD6B was found to be a poor prognostic marker and promotes tumor growth by upregulating MYC expression (PMID: 40533910). Additionally, evidence suggests its potential role in chemoresistance in ovarian cancer, which associates with poor survival outcomes (PMID: 39262489).

## 5. Translational Potential (across cancers)

### RFX5
Beyond breast cancer, RFX5's role in the activation of the Notch pathway has been noted in triple-negative breast cancer, where it facilitates tumor progression and may serve as a therapeutic target (PMID: 40220043).

### PARD6B
PARD6B's involvement in chemoresistance and its expression correlation with poor prognosis in gastrointestinal tumors highlights its role as a potential prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target in colorectal cancer (PMID: 40533910).
