# PAXIP1 and PI4KB: A Structured Report 

## 1. Background on Genes 
**PAXIP1 (PAX interacting protein 1)** is involved in DNA repair and transcriptional regulation, highlighting its role in various cancers, including hepatocellular and lung carcinomas. In hepatocellular carcinoma, PAXIP1 expression inversely correlates with survival outcomes. Its critical role in immune cell modulation and association with various prognostic markers underlines its potential as a prognostic biomarker (PMID: 39781045). PAXIP1 also acts as a tumor suppressor in lung cancer, potentially through its association with the cohesin complex (PMID: 39652422).

**PI4KB (Phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase beta)** primarily functions in the synthesis of phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate, a key lipid molecule involved in membrane identity and trafficking. While specific cancer associations of PI4KB weren't directly highlighted in the search task, its interaction with autophagy processes, especially in RAS-mutated cancers, positions it as a significant target in cancer biology. It is notably involved in a unique form of RAS-induced autophagy essential for tumor survival (PMID: 39988733, PMID: 40055523).

## 2. SL Evidence (with PMIDs) 
There are no available studies or abstracts explicitly mentioning synthetic lethality between PAXIP1 and PI4KB.

## 3. Drug Targets (with Open Targets data) 
Currently, there are no known drugs targeting PAXIP1 or PI4KB based on the Open Targets database. This highlights a potential area for future drug development and research, particularly given the roles of these genes in cancer-related pathways.

## 5. Translational Potential (across cancers)
PI4KB's role in the distinct regulation of RAS-driven non-canonical autophagy presents a promising target for therapeutic strategies in RAS-mutated cancers, as seen in pancreatic cancer models. The mechanism of PI4KB in such pathways, specifically through phosphorylation processes that impact autophagy, suggests potential crossover relevance in other RAS-related malignancies (PMID: 40055523).

## 6. Conclusion 
While PAXIP1 and PI4KB do not currently exhibit synthesized lethality in existing literature, their individual roles in cancer biology and potential as therapeutic targets in experimental models present intriguing avenues for further investigation. Targeting mechanisms involving PI4KB-related autophagy, particularly in RAS-mutated contexts, alongside the DNA repair-associated roles of PAXIP1, could pave the way for novel cancer treatment strategies.
