# Report on Gene Pair: CD47 – TBC1D28

## Background on Genes
CD47 has been identified as a significant player in cancer, acting as an immune checkpoint. It sends a "don't eat me" signal to prevent phagocytosis by macrophages, thus allowing cancer cells to evade the immune system. Recent studies indicate its application in therapies, particularly in targeting CD47 to enhance phagocytosis of tumor cells. Publications have highlighted its implications in various cancers, including acute myeloid leukemia (AML), breast cancer, and ovarian cancer ([PMID: 40634759](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40634759), [PMID: 40619860](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40619860), [PMID: 40384520](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40384520)).

No substantial evidence links TBC1D28 with cancer according to current available data from PubMed searches.

## SL Evidence (with PMIDs)
There is no evidence of synthetic lethality between CD47 and TBC1D28 in the current literature.

## Drug Targets (with Open Targets Data)
CD47 is targeted by several investigational drugs that act as immune checkpoint inhibitors:
- **Magrolimab**: A phase 2 and 3 investigational drug identified as a CD47 inhibitor, primarily aiming at hematologic malignancies such as acute myeloid leukemia and myelodysplastic syndrome ([NCT04313881](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04313881)).

TBC1D28: No drugs or inhibitors have been identified for TBC1D28.

## Translational Potential (across cancers)
CD47-targeting drugs, such as Magrolimab and Evorpacept, demonstrate utility across various cancer types beyond ovarian and breast, to include hematological malignancies and solid tumors like head and neck cancers ([NCT04675294](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04675294)).

## Conclusion
CD47 plays a notable role in immune evasion by cancer cells, making it a promising target for immune-modulatory therapies across a spectrum of malignancies. While active drug development and clinical trials focus on this target, TBC1D28 currently lacks similar evidence and therapeutic interest in the cancer domain.
