# TUSC3 and SUZ12 in Cancer: A Structured Report

## 1. Background on Genes

### TUSC3
TUSC3 (Tumor Suppressor Candidate 3) is implicated in the unfolded protein response in multiple myeloma, providing a survival advantage for cancer cells by overcoming stress from excessive proteosynthesis (PMID: 40088436). Studies have shown a significant correlation between TUSC3 deletion and the development of oral squamous cell carcinoma (PMID: 38992585). Additionally, TUSC3 plays a role in ovarian cancer and retinoblastoma, indicating its involvement in cellular proliferation and apoptosis (PMID: 32934674, PMID: 38707401).

### SUZ12
SUZ12 is a component of the Polycomb Repressive Complex 2, involved in methylating histone H3K27, and has been linked to cancers such as prostate and endometrial stromal tumors (PMID: 40525903, PMID: 36842409). Rearrangements involving SUZ12 (e.g., JAZF1-SUZ12) are frequently observed in low-grade endometrial stromal sarcoma, asserting its role in chromatin remodeling and its implication in various malignancies (PMID: 36842409, PMID: 32933053).

## 5. Translational Potential (across cancers)

Although both TUSC3 and SUZ12 have been involved in various cancers, there is limited evidence linking them in therapeutic strategies or synthetic lethality contexts across different cancer types.

## 6. Conclusion

Current literature and trial data provide substantial insights into the individual roles of TUSC3 and SUZ12 in cancer progression. However, there is a lack of evidence for their synthetic lethality, and no existing pharmacological inhibitors have been reported. More focused studies are needed to explore potential interactions between these genes that could be therapeutically exploited.
